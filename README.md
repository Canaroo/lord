# Beat Frets

A 16-bit rhythm game in the spirit of *Frets on Fire* and *Guitar Hero*. Play an original
chiptune song pack and bonus tracks, or import any MP3 and the game listens to it and
builds the note chart for you. Play on a keyboard, a game controller, or a phone or tablet
touchscreen.

**Play it:** https://canaroo.github.io/lord/

| Tier | Keys | What it's like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Easy** | 3 | Strong beats only. |
| **Medium** | 4 | Beats plus some off-beats, and long notes. |
| **Medium+** | 4 | Nearly Hard's note count and speed, still on four keys and without chords. |
| **Hard** | 5 | Off-beats, fast runs, two-button chords. |
| **Duo** | 2 | Hard's speed and density on two keys: quick notes alternate left-right. |

**Long notes** (Medium and up) have a tail: keep the key held until it ends. Hitting the
start counts like any note, and holding earns points. Let go and the rock meter drains every
second you're off it, with no points, until you press the key again to pick it back up. Your
streak isn't broken.

Controllers are detected automatically. Browsers only reveal a controller after one of its
buttons is pressed, so press any button and the on-screen labels switch to that pad's buttons.
The keyboard keeps working alongside it.

## Playing on a phone or tablet

The game switches to touch controls on its own when you tap the screen, and back again if you
press a key or a controller button.

- **Sideways: 4 lanes.** Tap the frets along the bottom with your fingers or thumbs.
- **Upright: 2 lanes**, one per thumb, like Duo.
- **Tiers:** Easy, Medium, Medium+ and Hard, in either direction. Touch charts are built for
  thumbs: quick notes switch hands, chords use one finger from each hand, and while one hand
  holds a long note, the other hand plays everything else. The timing window is slightly wider
  than on keys, and the overhit penalty is off, since a thumb can brush the glass.
- **Big tap pads, raised strike line:** on phones the notes are flat, lane-wide bars and the
  frets are lane-wide strike plates. The line sits about 72% of the way down when sideways (80%
  upright), and each lane carries on below it as a big pad down to the bottom edge. Rest your
  thumbs on the pads, below the line, and tap anywhere in a lane's column, so your thumbs never
  cover the notes coming in. A pressed pad lights up.
- **Long notes on a phone:** once you've grabbed one, that thumb can **slide anywhere**, off to
  the side or down off the frets, and it keeps holding. A glowing ring under your thumb, tied to
  its fret, shows it's still held, and the first few long notes in a song say so. A quick wobble
  that loses contact for a split second doesn't drop it. Phone charts put long notes on the outer
  lanes and keep the lane beside them clear, so the other thumb never has to squeeze in next to
  the held one.
- **Tap assist:** hit areas are a little wider than they look. A tap that lands just inside the
  lane next to a note (or on a lane your other thumb is holding) counts for the note.
- **Nothing covers the notes.** The multiplier and streak sit to the left of the far end of the
  highway, and PERFECT / GOOD / MISS to the right. Banners like *OVERDRIVE!* appear in the sky
  above it.
- **Everything you need is on the highway.** The left edge of the highway is the **OD meter**
  and the right edge is the **rock meter**, both filling up from the frets. Your score sits big and centred just
  above the highway, rolling up as you play, with your stars and personal-best pace under it.
- **Overdrive:** when it's ready, a chime plays, *SWIPE UP!* sweeps across the highway, big
  arrows climb up both sides of the highway, and the frets and track glow cyan until you use it. **Flick any finger up** off the frets to fire it.
  Your thumbs never have to leave the bottom of the screen. **Pause:** the **II** button, or the
  phone's **Back** button.
- **Menus:** every menu has a **BACK** button at the bottom, and the phone's own Back button
  (or an edge swipe on iPhone) steps back one menu instead of leaving the game.
- **Turning the phone mid-song** pauses it until you turn it back, because the lanes would change.
- Touch scores have their own leaderboards (**TOUCH 4** and **TOUCH 2** tabs), so thumbs never
  compete against keyboards.
- **First game:** the game offers a quick tap-along timing check. Phone speakers and Bluetooth
  earbuds lag differently, so it's worth doing; redo it any time in **Options**.
- **Vibration** on misses and streaks can be turned on in **Options** (Android only).
- The screen stays awake while you play, and **Press Start** goes full screen.
- **iPhone:** if there's no sound, flip the silent switch off.

**Install it like an app:** open the game in Chrome on Android and choose **Add to Home screen**
(or *Install app*), or in Safari on iPhone/iPad tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**. It then
opens full screen with its own icon and works offline: the song pack, the game, and any bonus
track you've already played. With a connection it always loads the newest version; if an update
arrives while it's open, it switches over the next time you're on the title screen or main menu.

## How to play

Hit each note as it crosses the line.

- **Streak multiplier:** every 10 notes in a row raises it, up to ×4.
- **Overdrive (OD meter, left):** fills as you hit notes, with a bonus every 50-note streak.
  Once it's half full, trigger it for double points (up to ×8). A full meter lasts 16 seconds.
- **Rock meter (right):** rises on hits and drops on misses. If it empties, the song fails.
  You can turn on **No-fail mode** in Options.
- **Grades:** S, A, B, C or D by accuracy. "Full combo" means you missed nothing.

**Timing feels off?** Go to **Options → Auto-calibrate timing** and tap along with the clicks.
Bluetooth headphones usually need this.

## Feel and feedback

- **The stage follows your streak.** At ×1 it's calm and dim. At ×2 the lights pulse and the
  crowd bobs. At ×3 the rails glow and the crowd raises its arms. At ×4 and in overdrive, lasers
  sweep, stars streak past, the edges glow and the crowd jumps. Break a long streak and the
  stage powers down for a moment.
- **The guitarist** next to the highway wears the current player's colour. He head-bobs,
  head-bangs and jumps as you build a streak, slumps when you lose it, and sits down if the song
  fails.
- **The music reacts.** It gets muffled as the rock meter runs low, ducks on a missed note, and
  brightens during overdrive.
- **On screen:** ten pips around the multiplier fill toward the next level, and points pop up
  from each fret. A gold frame stays lit while a full combo is alive.
- **Stars:** every song and tier is rated 1–5 stars by score. Five stars with a full combo turns
  them gold. Your stars show while you play, on the song screen and on your player profile.
- **Pace:** replay a song and the HUD shows how far ahead of or behind your personal best you are.
- **Results:** a graph of how each part of the song went, your average timing (with a one-press
  fix if you're consistently early or late), and a suggestion for what to try next.
- **Stray presses** on Medium and up break your streak, so mashing doesn't pay. Easy stays
  forgiving, and **Options → Overhit penalty** turns it off everywhere.
- **Pausing** stops the song. Resuming rewinds a second and counts you back in.
- **Saved music:** imported songs are kept in your browser and listed under *Your music*, one
  click to play or delete. Song lists play a short preview of whatever's highlighted.

## Leaderboards and sharing

Everything is saved in your browser on this device. Nothing is uploaded.

- **Players:** each person enters arcade-style initials and gets their own scores. Switch
  players from the main menu.
- **Leaderboards:** each song and tier has its own board with every player's best score.
  Failed runs don't count.
- **Sharing:** after a song, **Share** makes a pixel-art score card (copy the link, save the
  image, or use your device's share menu). A friend who opens the link sees your score and can
  add it to their leaderboard as a **rival**.
- **Fair comparisons:** the **chiptune pack** charts are built from the composition itself,
  and **bonus tracks** ship with pre-built charts, so everyone plays identical notes. For **imported MP3s**, two people's copies of a song are
  usually different files, so the game matches them by how they *sound* (length, tempo and
  the shape of the song's loudness over time). Your friend's score then lands on the same board.
- **Backups:** **Save data → Export** downloads your save as a file. Import it on another
  computer or browser to bring your players and scores along. Importing merges and never
  overwrites.

Share links have a checksum, so a hand-edited link shows as **unverified**. Like any browser
game, a determined player could still fake a score, so treat rival scores as friendly bragging.

## Bonus tracks

Recorded songs can ship with the game in the `songs/` folder. Each one downloads only when
it's picked, and comes with a pre-built chart made by `npm run build-charts`. Browsers decode
MP3s with slightly different start delays, so on load the game listens to the first 30 seconds
and slides the chart into line with the audio. See [`songs/README.md`](songs/README.md) for
how to add songs. Only add music you have the right to share: the site is public.

## How the auto-charting works

Imported songs are analysed in the browser (`js/analyzer.js`):

1. **Per-band spectral flux:** the song is split into ~12 ms frames and run through an FFT.
   For 5 frequency bands, the game measures sudden rises in energy (drum hits, plucked notes).
2. **Onsets:** peaks that stand out from the local average become candidate notes, each with
   a strength and a 5-band "fingerprint".
3. **Tempo:** autocorrelation of the onset curve gives the BPM.
4. **Beat tracking:** dynamic programming (Ellis, 2007) finds a steady sequence of beats.

Then `js/chartgen.js` builds the chart. It snaps onsets to a beat/8th/16th-note grid, keeps
the strong ones (beats first), enforces a minimum gap between notes (300 ms on Easy, 100 ms on
Hard), and picks lanes by frequency: kick and bass on the left, hi-hats and cymbals on the
right. It then spreads notes so no single fret hogs the chart. On Duo, notes closer than
0.3 s alternate keys.

**Long notes** come from how long each sound rings. The analyzer measures, per frequency band,
how long the level stays up after a hit. Drums die away in a fraction of a second, while held
synths, vocals and chords ring on. Sounds that ring for half a second or more can become long
notes. Their lane is kept clear for the length of the tail, so other notes go to other keys
and you play them while holding. On the chiptune songs, long notes use the composed note
lengths exactly.

Song pack tracks (`js/songs.js`) are written as patterns and synthesised with a 16-bit-style
sound palette: pulse-wave leads with SNES-style echo, triangle bass and noise drums. Their
charts come from the notes themselves, with the melody walking across the frets.

## Development

There is no build step. The page is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

```
js/analyzer.js   onset detection, tempo, beat tracking
js/chartgen.js   analysis → note charts for every tier, keys and touch
js/songs.js      chiptune song pack (composition, synthesis, charts)
js/bonus.js      bonus tracks: lining pre-built charts up with the decoded audio
tools/build-charts.js   builds bonus-track charts (needs ffmpeg)
js/render.js     the 16-bit stage: highway, crowd, lights, HUD
js/game.js       timing, judging, scoring, rock meter, overdrive
js/input.js      keyboard, Gamepad API and touch input
sw.js            service worker (offline play); manifest.webmanifest + icons/ make it installable
js/sfx.js        chiptune sound effects, volume buses, reactive music filters
js/library.js    imported songs saved in the browser (IndexedDB)
js/save.js       players, scores, leaderboards, share links, song matching
js/share.js      score card image and challenge links
js/main.js       menus and app flow
```

```bash
npm start                         # serve locally on http://localhost:8080
npm test                          # unit tests (plain Node, no dependencies)
npm run build-charts              # build charts for MP3s in songs/
npm i -D playwright && npm run e2e -- [song.mp3]   # full browser test
E2E_ONLY=touch npm run e2e        # just the phone checks
```

The end-to-end test drives the game with a simulated Xbox controller. It enters initials,
navigates menus with the D-pad, plays a full song (overdrive included), checks the
leaderboard, shares the score, opens the link as a friend in a separate browser, accepts the
challenge, fails a song on purpose, pauses with Start, and imports an MP3. It then switches to
a simulated phone: installs the app, plays sideways on 4 lanes and upright on 2 with finger
taps, swipes up for overdrive, uses the BACK buttons and the phone's Back button, taps pause, and
turns the phone mid-song.
