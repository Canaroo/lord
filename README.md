# Beat Frets

A 16-bit rhythm game in the spirit of *Frets on Fire* and *Guitar Hero*. Play an original
chiptune song pack and bonus tracks, or import any MP3 and the game listens to it and
builds the note chart for you. Play on a keyboard, a game controller, or a phone or tablet
touchscreen.

**Play it:** https://canaroo.github.io/lord/

| Tier | Keys | What it's like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Easy** | 3 | Strong beats only. |
| **Medium** | 4 | Beats plus some off-beats, and long notes. |
| **Medium+** | 4 | Nearly Hard's note count and speed, still on four keys and without chords. |
| **Hard** | 5 | Off-beats, fast runs, two-button chords. |
| **Duo** | 2 | Hard's speed and density on two keys: quick notes alternate left-right. |

**Long notes** (Medium and up) have a tail: keep the key held until it ends. Hitting the
start counts like any note, and holding earns points. Let go and the rock meter drains every
second you're off it, with no points, until you press the key again to pick it back up. Your
streak isn't broken.

buttons is pressed, so press any button and the on-screen labels switch to that pad's buttons.
The keyboard keeps working alongside it.

## Tiles mode

A second way to play every song, in the style of *Magic Tiles*. Pick **Tiles Mode** on the main
menu, or the **TILES** switch on any song's screen.

- **Hold the phone upright** (Tiles is upright only). Four columns fill the screen and dark tiles
  fall straight down.
  Tap a tile's column as its coloured edge reaches the line near the bottom (a little early is
  fine). A tapped tile floods with colour from its border inward.
- **Long tiles** have coloured side rails, a dashed track and a start pad with an arrow. Press and
  hold: the tile stops at the line and fills with colour from the bottom to the top, with a rising
  note at each quarter, and explodes when it's full. Let go early and the colour drains away.
- **Tap START to begin.** The song starts the moment you tap the big START tile. Before that you
  can drag the line up or down to where your thumbs like it; it's remembered, and it's also in
  **Options → Tiles line height**.
- **Two hearts.** A tile that slips past, or a tap on an empty column, costs one. Each new lap
  gives one back.
- **One second chance.** The first time you lose your last heart, the song rewinds three seconds
  (the tile you missed comes back) and counts you in. The gold **+1** by the hearts shows it's
  still available. Lose your last heart again and the run ends.
- **Tiles you tap together** are joined by a glowing bridge, with a flashing outline and "x2".
- **Quick repeats in one column** are shortened so they never overlap, and dots count down the
  taps left in the run.
- **It keeps going.** When the song ends it loops, 22% faster every lap (up to 2.5x speed).
- **Every tap counts:** each tile plays a chime (its column's note, with a sparkle on PERFECT),
  fills with colour, presses down a touch, and sends a beam of light up its column. The board pulses to the beat and
  flashes every 25 tiles in a row.
- **Stars and crowns:** three stars through the first lap, then a crown for every lap after.
- **Easy, Medium and Hard** use the same note charts as the phone game. Tiles scores have their
  own leaderboard tab. On a keyboard, the columns are `A` `S` `D` `F`.

## Getting around

The menus look like an early-90s arcade cabinet, and every screen fills the display:

- **Tabs along the bottom:** **Play** (the two games, Beat Frets and Tiles, as big cards),
  **Boards**, **Player** and **Options**. Save data lives inside Options.
- **The marquee across the top** says what screen you're on and where you are
  (*BEAT FRETS · 4 LANES › PIXEL OVERTURE*), with a round **◀ back** button on the left and a
  red **home** button on the right.
- **The screen's actions sit in a dock at the bottom**, within thumb reach. The main one is red
  (PLAY AGAIN, PLAY THIS SONG); the others are blue.
- **What you can press always looks pressable:** arcade buttons with a bevel that push down,
  list rows with a **›**, lit switches and chips for filters, and ◀ ▶ pickers. Headings are flat
  gold text and section labels are small cyan caps between lines, so they never look like buttons.
- **Leaderboards** open on the **World** board (when it's switched on), with chips for how the
  song was played (Keys, Sideways, Upright, Tiles). Scores are cards
  with gold, silver and bronze medals, and your own best is pinned under the list. Every
  difficulty has its own tab, all in view and colour-coded (EASY, MEDIUM, MEDIUM+, HARD), each
  saying who leads it or *NO ONE YET*; a heading says which board you're on (*EASY LEADERBOARD ·
  SIDEWAYS*).
- **The Boards tab starts with the same difficulty tabs** (and Keys / Sideways / Upright / Tiles).
  Each tab counts the songs that have a leader there (*👑 3 SONGS* or *NO ONE YET*), and every
  category and song below shows who leads that difficulty and their score, so you can see where
  there's a record to chase without opening anything. A song's board opens on the difficulty picked.

- **Songs come in categories** (Chiptune Classics, Spooky, Rock, Epic Adventure, Space, Carnival, Salsa, Hip Hop, Bonus tracks)
  that start closed, so even a long list fits on a phone. Each heading says how many of its
  records are claimed and by whom (*👑 7 OF 12 CLAIMED · YOU 2 · ZED 4 · AMY 1*), or *NO RECORDS
  YET · BE THE FIRST*. Tap one to open it (the others close), and the game remembers it for next
  time. Inside, every song shows who leads each difficulty (*👑 E ZED · M YOU · H —*), or *NO LEADER
  YET*. A record counts per difficulty, for the way you're playing (Keys, Sideways, Upright or
  Tiles). It's the world record when the world boards are on and reachable, otherwise the best on
  this device. **Boards** groups its songs the same way, with a lit gold trophy on songs that
  have a leader.

Pick a game, then a song (Song pack or Your music), then a difficulty. After a song, **SONGS**
takes you back to the list.

## Playing on a phone or tablet

The game switches to touch controls on its own when you tap the screen, and back again if you
press a key or a controller button.

- **Beat Frets follows how you hold the phone:** sideways is 4 lanes, upright is 2. No question
  asked: turn the phone and the song screen switches. A **SIDEWAYS · 4 LANES / UPRIGHT · 2 LANES**
  switch on the song screen shows which you're on; tap the other to change (an installed app turns
  the screen for you; otherwise a hint asks you to turn the phone). Tiles is upright only.
- **Home tells you what each way up offers.** Upright: Beat Frets (marked *UPRIGHT · 2 LANES*,
  turn sideways for 4) and Tiles (*UPRIGHT ONLY*). Sideways: only Beat Frets, on 4 lanes, with a
  note to turn the phone upright for Tiles. Turn the phone sideways in Tiles' menus and you're in
  Beat Frets on 4 lanes.
- **Menus work either way up.** Only a song in progress needs the phone held its way: turn it
  mid-song and the song pauses, offering **PLAY UPRIGHT/SIDEWAYS INSTEAD** (the same song on the
  lanes for how you're holding it now) or **BACK TO THE SONG**.

- **Sideways: 4 lanes.** Tap the frets along the bottom with your fingers or thumbs.
- **Upright: 2 lanes**, one per thumb, like Duo.
- **Tiers:** Easy, Medium, Medium+ and Hard, in either direction. Touch charts are built for
  thumbs: quick notes switch hands, chords use one finger from each hand, and while one hand
  holds a long note, the other hand plays everything else. The timing window is slightly wider
  than on keys, since a thumb can brush the glass.
- **Big tap pads, raised strike line:** on phones the notes are short, wide arcade-button caps centred on
  the beat, so the strike line passes through the middle of each one. A note's length doesn't
  change how easy it is to hit (the whole lane is the target), so blocks stay short and shrink
  further when notes in a lane come close together, always leaving a dark gap between them. The
  frets are lane-wide arcade buttons that press down and light up when you tap. The line sits about 72% of the way down when sideways (80%
  upright), and each lane carries on below it as a big pad down to the bottom edge. Rest your
  thumbs on the pads, below the line, and tap anywhere in a lane's column, so your thumbs never
  cover the notes coming in. A pressed pad lights up.
- **Options wait for SAVE:** changing a setting (a slider or a switch) doesn't take effect
  straight away. The change lights up gold with what it was before, and a save bar appears:
  **UNDO** or **SAVE**. Leaving Options with unsaved changes asks *KEEP YOUR CHANGES?* (save,
  discard, or keep editing), so a slider nudged by accident changes nothing. Volume changes play
  a preview while you slide.
- **Your strike line height:** drag the line to where your thumbs rest (offered after your first
  phone game). Change it any time in **Options** (*Strike line, sideways / upright* or *Move
  strike line*). Sideways and upright are saved separately.
- **Long notes on a phone:** once you've grabbed one, that thumb can **slide anywhere**, off to
  the side or down off the frets, and it keeps holding. A glowing ring under your thumb, tied to
  its fret, shows it's still held, and the first few long notes in a song say so. A quick wobble
  that loses contact for a split second doesn't drop it. Phone charts put long notes on the outer
  lanes and keep the lane beside them clear, so the other thumb never has to squeeze in next to
  the held one.
- **Slides:** some phone long notes have a striped, white-edged tail that curves into the next
  lane, and sometimes back again (CHUNITHM / Arcaea style). Hit the start, then slide your thumb
  along with it. A bracket on the strike line shows where your thumb should be. It's forgiving:
  being within about a lane of the path counts, and a quick wobble doesn't drop it. Sideways, a
  slide stays on its own thumb's half; upright, the other thumb gets a rest while it crosses.
  Keyboard and controller charts don't have slides.
- **Tap assist:** hit areas are a little wider than they look. A tap that lands just inside the
  lane next to a note (or on a lane your other thumb is holding) counts for the note.
- **Nothing covers the notes.** The multiplier and streak sit to the left of the far end of the
  highway, and PERFECT / GOOD / MISS to the right. Banners like *OVERDRIVE!* appear in the sky
  above it.
- **Everything you need is on the highway.** The left edge of the highway is the **OD meter**
  and the right edge is the **rock meter**, both filling up from the frets. Your score sits big and centred just
  above the highway, rolling up as you play, with your stars and personal-best pace under it.
- **Overdrive:** when it's ready, a chime plays, *SWIPE UP!* sweeps across the highway, big
  arrows climb up both sides of the highway, and the frets and track glow cyan until you use it. **Flick any finger up** off the frets to fire it.
  Your thumbs never have to leave the bottom of the screen. **Pause:** the **II** button, or the
  phone's **Back** button.
- **Menus:** every menu has a **BACK** button at the bottom, and the phone's own Back button
  (or an edge swipe on iPhone) steps back one menu instead of leaving the game.
- **Turning the phone mid-song** pauses it until you turn it back, because the lanes would change.
- Touch scores have their own leaderboards (**TOUCH 4** and **TOUCH 2** tabs), so thumbs never
  compete against keyboards.
- **First game:** starts straight away. Afterwards the results offer a 10-second **TIMING TEST**
  (phone speakers and Bluetooth earbuds lag differently) and **MOVE THE LINE** (to where your
  thumbs rest); they come back whenever a game's hits go astray. Both are in **Options** too.
- **Vibration** on misses and streaks can be turned on in **Options** (Android only).
- The screen stays awake while you play, and **Press Start** goes full screen.
- **iPhone:** if there's no sound, flip the silent switch off.

**Install it like an app:** open the game in Chrome on Android and choose **Add to Home screen**
(or *Install app*), or in Safari on iPhone/iPad tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**. It then
opens full screen with its own icon and works offline: the song pack, the game, and any bonus
track you've already played. With a connection it always loads the newest version; if an update
arrives while it's open, it switches over the next time you're on the title screen or main menu.

## How to play

Hit each note as it crosses the line.

- **Streak multiplier:** every 10 notes in a row raises it, up to ×4.
- **Overdrive (OD meter, left):** fills as you hit notes, with a bonus every 50-note streak.
  Once it's half full, trigger it for double points (up to ×8). A full meter lasts 16 seconds.
- **Rock meter (right):** rises on hits and drops on misses. If it empties, the song fails.
  You can turn on **No-fail mode** in Options to play any song to the end. No-fail runs are
  **practice**: they count for your own stars, but never go on a leaderboard (this device or the
  world), can't be shared, and can't win a challenge. The song screen, the song's intro and the
  results all say *NO-FAIL PRACTICE* so nobody is surprised.
- **Grades:** S, A, B, C or D by accuracy. "Full combo" means you missed nothing.

**Timing feels off?** Go to **Options → Auto-calibrate timing** and tap along with the clicks.
Bluetooth headphones usually need this.

## Feel and feedback

- **Notes that arrive together** are joined by a bar, so chords read at a glance.

- **The stage follows your streak.** At ×1 it's calm and dim. At ×2 the lights pulse and the
  crowd bobs. At ×3 the rails glow and the crowd raises its arms. At ×4 and in overdrive, lasers
  sweep, stars streak past, the edges glow and the crowd jumps. Break a long streak and the
  stage powers down for a moment.
- **The guitarist** next to the highway wears the current player's colour. He head-bobs,
  head-bangs and jumps as you build a streak, slumps when you lose it, and sits down if the song
  fails.
- **The music reacts.** It gets muffled as the rock meter runs low, ducks on a missed note, and
  brightens during overdrive.
- **On screen:** ten pips around the multiplier fill toward the next level, and points pop up
  from each fret. A gold frame stays lit while a full combo is alive.
- **Stars:** every song and tier is rated 1–5 stars by score. Five stars with a full combo turns
  them gold. Your stars show while you play, on the song screen and on your player profile.
- **Pace:** replay a song and the HUD shows how far ahead of or behind your personal best you are.
- **Results:** a graph of how each part of the song went, your average timing (with a one-press
  fix if you're consistently early or late), and a suggestion for what to try next.
- **Stray presses** never cost you anything: only the notes count.
- **Pausing** stops the song. Resuming rewinds a second and counts you back in.
- **Saved music:** imported songs are kept in your browser and listed under *Your music*, one
  click to play or delete.
- **Previews:** every song has a ▶ button. Built-in songs are made on the phone the first time
  (10–60 seconds): the button fills up as the song is made, blinks READY, then plays a 15-second
  clip of the chorus. Tap several and they're made one after another; tap a playing preview to
  stop it. Songs already made and your own music are ready straight away; bonus tracks download first.

## Leaderboards and sharing

Everything is saved in your browser on this device. If world boards are switched on (see
below), your initials and best scores are also posted there.

- **Players:** each person enters arcade-style initials (on a phone, from a grid of big letters)
  and gets their own scores. Switch
  players from the main menu.
- **Leaderboards:** each song and tier has its own board with every player's best score.
  Failed runs don't count.
- **Sharing:** after a song, **Share** makes a pixel-art score card (copy the link, save the
  image, or use your device's share menu). A friend who opens the link sees your score and can
  add it to their leaderboard as a **rival**.
- **Fair comparisons:** the **chiptune pack** charts are built from the composition itself,
  and **bonus tracks** ship with pre-built charts, so everyone plays identical notes. For **imported MP3s**, two people's copies of a song are
  usually different files, so the game matches them by how they *sound* (length, tempo and
  the shape of the song's loudness over time). Your friend's score then lands on the same board.
- **Backups:** **Save data → Export** downloads your save as a file. Import it on another
  computer or browser to bring your players and scores along. Importing merges and never
  overwrites.

Share links have a checksum, so a hand-edited link shows as **unverified**. Like any browser
game, a determined player could still fake a score, so treat rival scores as friendly bragging.

### World leaderboards

Every board has two tabs: **This device** and **World**, for Beat Frets and Tiles alike.

**You choose what gets posted.** After a song, the results screen checks the world board and
says how the run compares (*Not on the world board yet. This run would rank #4.*). Tap **POST TO
WORLD** to put it up; play again or leave and nothing is posted. The button only appears when
posting would change something: your first score on that board, or a new best. Each player keeps
one entry per board, and it can only go up, so a bad run never replaces a good one. If you'd
rather not be asked, turn on **Options → Post new bests automatically**.

Tiles scores have no fixed ceiling (the song loops, and each lap's tiles are worth more), so the
world board's anti-cheat limit for Tiles grows with the number of laps played.

The world boards are fair because a score only ranks against people who played **the same notes**:

- **Song pack and bonus tracks** have identical charts for everyone, so each song and tier has
  one world board.
- **Imported MP3s** get a board named after their **chart**: a fingerprint of the notes,
  measured from the first note. Two versions of a song (a different rip, a remaster, a cover)
  usually chart differently, so their scores don't mix.
- **Challenges:** on a world board, every other player's score has a red **⚔ CHALLENGE** button
  (#1 wears the **LEADER** crown). It opens a *HERE COMES A NEW CHALLENGER!* pop-up: you vs them,
  your rank and theirs, and the score to beat. The song loads behind it, and on a phone it waits
  for you to turn the phone the right way (Android turns itself where it can; iPhones can't be
  turned by a web page, so it asks). **FIGHT!** starts a live race: instead of your personal best,
  the HUD shows who leads (with a crown) and the gap, green when you're ahead and red when behind,
  measured against their score at the same moment of their run (each posted score saves its
  score-over-time; older scores use an estimate, marked ~). Win, and a **YOU BEAT …!** pop-up
  offers to post; posted, you take their place, and your card shows **⚔ BEAT …** for a week.
  Lose or tie, and it offers a **REMATCH**. Races are only between the same controls: keyboard
  scores are challenged from a computer, phone scores from a phone.
- **Tiles challenges** work the same way, from any device (Tiles has the same four columns
  everywhere; on a phone it's played upright). Because Tiles loops and speeds up identically for
  everyone, the race compares your score with theirs at the same point of the run, across laps;
  once their run ended, you're racing their final score.
- **Records to beat:** in the song lists, a red **⚔ 2 TO BEAT** badge marks songs where someone
  holds a world record you haven't matched (on the difficulties for how you're playing: keys,
  sideways, upright or Tiles). On the difficulty cards, each tier shows its world record (👑 and
  the score) and your gap to it: red and negative when you're behind (a tier you've never played
  counts as 0, so the gap is the whole record), green when the record is yours.
- **"You've been beaten":** when someone wins a challenge against your score and posts it, you
  get a message. The next time you open the game, the home screen pops up *YOU'VE BEEN BEATEN!*
  with who beat you, on which song, and the scores; tap one for a **REMATCH** (as the player who
  was beaten, if several share the device). Messages are private to your device and cleared once
  seen. A message can only be sent with a real posted score that's higher than yours.
- **Players' music:** the Boards tab lists imported songs other players have posted (newest
  first), even ones you don't have. Open one to see its world board. To challenge a score on a
  song you don't have, the versus pop-up asks you to **import your own copy** (any version: a
  different rip, a remaster); once imported, it goes straight back into the challenge. The game
  also finds your copy by itself if you already have the song in Your music, matched by sound.
- **Naming songs:** the first time an imported song goes on the world board, the game asks what
  it's called (the file name is suggested), because everyone will see that name.
- **Challenge links:** a shared score links to its world board, so a friend who opens it goes
  straight to the challenge (importing their copy first, for an imported song).
- **Imported songs:** a challenge plays the board's own notes over your copy. For an imported song, the
  World tab also lists **other versions of this song** that players have posted. Pick one and
  you play **their exact notes over your own copy** of the song. The game lines their chart up
  with your audio (any difference in where the song starts, up to 15 seconds), and your score
  goes on their board. Nobody sends an MP3. If their notes don't match your copy well, the game
  warns you that it may be a different version.

**Turning them on** (they're off until a Firebase project is connected):

1. In the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com/), use an existing project or
   create one, then add a **Web app** and copy its config.
2. Paste the config into `js/firebase-config.js` (`window.BEAT_FRETS_FIREBASE = { … }`).
3. **Build → Firestore Database:** create the database, then paste `firestore.rules` into the
   **Rules** tab and publish. If the project already serves another game, add the
   `beatfrets_boards` block next to that game's rules rather than replacing them.
4. **Build → Authentication → Sign-in method:** enable **Anonymous**. Each device signs in
   silently; no accounts or emails are involved.
5. **Authentication → Settings → Authorized domains:** make sure the site's domain (for example
   `canaroo.github.io`) is listed.

The Firebase web config is safe to publish; the security rules are what protect the data. They
only let a device write its own entries, only raise a score, and cap scores by the chart's note
count. Scores can still be faked by someone determined, as in any browser game.

## Bonus tracks

Recorded songs can ship with the game in the `songs/` folder. Each one downloads only when
it's picked, and comes with a pre-built chart made by `npm run build-charts`. Browsers decode
MP3s with slightly different start delays, so on load the game listens to the first 30 seconds
and slides the chart into line with the audio. See [`songs/README.md`](songs/README.md) for
how to add songs. Only add music you have the right to share: the site is public.

## How the auto-charting works

Imported songs are analysed in the browser (`js/analyzer.js`):

1. **Per-band spectral flux:** the song is split into ~12 ms frames and run through an FFT.
   For 5 frequency bands, the game measures sudden rises in energy (drum hits, plucked notes).
2. **Onsets:** peaks that stand out from the local average become candidate notes, each with
   a strength and a 5-band "fingerprint".
3. **Tempo:** autocorrelation of the onset curve gives the BPM.
4. **Beat tracking:** dynamic programming (Ellis, 2007) finds a steady sequence of beats.

Then `js/chartgen.js` builds the chart. It snaps onsets to a beat/8th/16th-note grid, keeps
the strong ones (beats first), enforces a minimum gap between notes (300 ms on Easy, 100 ms on
Hard), and picks lanes by frequency: kick and bass on the left, hi-hats and cymbals on the
right. It then spreads notes so no single fret hogs the chart. On Duo, notes closer than
0.3 s alternate keys.

**Long notes** come from how long each sound rings. The analyzer measures, per frequency band,
how long the level stays up after a hit. Drums die away in a fraction of a second, while held
synths, vocals and chords ring on. Sounds that ring for half a second or more can become long
notes. Their lane is kept clear for the length of the tail, so other notes go to other keys
and you play them while holding. On the chiptune songs, long notes use the composed note
lengths exactly.

Song pack tracks (`js/songs.js`) are written as patterns and synthesised with a 16-bit-style
sound palette: pulse-wave leads with SNES-style echo, triangle bass and noise drums. Each song
can pick its own sounds (`voice`): a drawbar **organ** with vibrato, a square lead, a **brass**
lead whose tone opens as it swells, toms or deep **timpani**, and distorted **power-chord
guitar** (`gtr` patterns: `x` palm-muted chug, `o` open chord), a glowing space **synth** with
twinkling **bell** arpeggios, and a steam **calliope** whose melodies can slide chromatically
(`leadScale: 'chromatic'`). Their charts come from the notes
themselves, with the melody walking across the frets.

Every theme has three songs, from a gentle one to a tough one. Each can be played on any
difficulty, but the song itself is naturally easier or harder, and a badge says so:
**ROOKIE** (green), **PRO** (gold) and **BOSS** (red, with a skull). A ROOKIE song stays
approachable even on Hard; a BOSS song is a challenge even on Easy.

To add a song, give it a `theme` and a `tier` (`rookie`, `pro` or `boss`); the lists show each
theme's songs in the order they appear in `js/songs.js`. As a guide, on Easy a ROOKIE song has
about 1.7 notes a second, PRO about 2.1 and BOSS 2.6 or more (`npm test` prints nothing about
this, but the numbers are easy to check with ChartGen).

With Claude Code, the **add-song** skill (`.claude/skills/add-song/`) does all of this from a
loose idea ("a spooky rookie song about a haunted carnival"): it composes the song, tunes its
density to the tier, runs the tests, renders an MP3 preview to listen to, and waits for your OK
before publishing. It can also start a new theme or retune a song. Its two scripts work on their
own too: `node .claude/skills/add-song/scripts/song-stats.js` prints every song's notes per second
and checks each theme's ROOKIE → PRO → BOSS order.

Songs are synthesised at 32 kHz (the SNES's own output rate) the first time they're played on a
device, with a progress bar (roughly 10 to 60 seconds, longer for faster, busier songs), and
then kept in the browser so every later load is instant (and works offline). A song is rebuilt
automatically if its composition changes. A kept song takes about 4 to 7 MB: the mix is stored
at full quality, and the gentle stereo panning more compactly. The song data itself is tiny
(about 4 KB a song), so the practical limit on how many songs the pack can hold is storage and
first-play building time, not download size: 50 or more is comfortable.

## Development

There is no build step. The page is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

```
js/analyzer.js   onset detection, tempo, beat tracking
js/chartgen.js   analysis → note charts for every tier, keys and touch
js/songs.js      chiptune song pack (composition, synthesis, charts)
js/bonus.js      bonus tracks: lining pre-built charts up with the decoded audio
tools/build-charts.js   builds bonus-track charts (needs ffmpeg)
js/render.js     the 16-bit stage: highway, crowd, lights, HUD
js/game.js       timing, judging, scoring, rock meter, overdrive
js/tiles.js      Tiles mode: looping, speeding-up laps, lives, its own board
js/input.js      keyboard, Gamepad API and touch input
sw.js            service worker (offline play); manifest.webmanifest + icons/ make it installable
js/sfx.js        chiptune sound effects, volume buses, reactive music filters
js/library.js    imported songs saved in the browser (IndexedDB)
js/save.js       players, scores, leaderboards, share links, song matching
js/share.js      score card image and challenge links
js/world.js      world leaderboards (Firebase): chart-keyed boards, chart alignment for challenges
js/firebase-config.js   paste your Firebase web config here; firestore.rules has the matching rules
js/main.js       menus and app flow
```

```bash
npm start                         # serve locally on http://localhost:8080
npm test                          # unit tests (plain Node, no dependencies)
npm run build-charts              # build charts for MP3s in songs/
npm i -D playwright && npm run e2e -- [song.mp3]   # full browser test
E2E_ONLY=touch npm run e2e        # just the phone checks
E2E_ONLY=world npm run e2e        # just the world boards (with an in-page stand-in for Firebase)
```

The end-to-end test drives the game with a simulated Xbox controller. It enters initials,
navigates menus with the D-pad, plays a full song (overdrive included), checks the
leaderboard, shares the score, opens the link as a friend in a separate browser, accepts the
challenge, fails a song on purpose, pauses with Start, and imports an MP3. It then switches to
a simulated phone: installs the app, plays sideways on 4 lanes and upright on 2 with finger
taps, swipes up for overdrive, uses the BACK buttons and the phone's Back button, taps pause, and
turns the phone mid-song.
